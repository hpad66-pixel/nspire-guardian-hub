The sanitary sewer extension is physically built. Two things remain, and they are different in kind. The first is administrative: transferring the asset to the City of Opa-Locka through a formal conveyance package. The second is operational: putting a permanent flow meter at the point where wastewater leaves the property, so that Glorieta — the system's largest single discharger — can prove exactly what it sends to the City's pump station.

Why this matters

Until conveyance closes, the sewer remains the owner's asset, the owner's maintenance obligation, and the owner's warranty exposure. Until the meter is in, every flow question is an argument instead of a fact.

Conveyance & Close-Out to the City of Opa-Locka

Transfer the completed sewer extension to the City so it becomes the City's asset to own, operate and maintain.

This is an administrative project, and it deserves to be run like a project — it has a defined scope, a set of dependencies on five outside parties, and a fee. It is not a punch list item. Until the City formally accepts the system, the owner continues to hold the asset, the maintenance duty and the warranty exposure.

Scope of Work
  • Run the City of Opa-Locka Utilities Conveyance Checklist to completion, item by item, with a named owner and a date against every line.
  • Coordinate the Engineer of Record deliverables: signed and sealed record drawings, infiltration and exfiltration certification, Engineer's Final Payment Confirmation, FDEP sewer certification, General Permit and In-Service Letter, latitude and longitude detail sheet, and the record-drawing disk.
  • Coordinate the surveyor's signed and sealed as-built survey (PDF plus editable CAD) and reconcile it against the record drawings.
  • Coordinate the contractor's CCTV video of the constructed gravity sewer, the Sewer Report and the Master Utility Sheet, plus field completion and restoration for the City's preliminary inspection.
  • Close the contractor package: punch list completion, final lien waivers, material certifications, test results, permit close-outs, and asphalt and sod final quantities.
  • Execute and file the three twelve-month warranties by building group — Building 3 South with Building 4; Building 5 with Building 6; and Building 3 North.
  • Support owner's counsel on the legal and security items: Attorney's and Owner's Affidavits, Warranty Deed and Bill of Sale, UCC subordination, and the Utilities Performance Security.
  • Assemble, transmit and track the acceptance package through City review to written acceptance.
Deliverables
  • Completed Utilities Conveyance Checklist with evidence attached to every line
  • Transmittal package to the City of Opa-Locka
  • Executed warranty set (three building groups)
  • Written City acceptance and asset transfer confirmation
  • Retainage release documentation per the contract schedule

Master Effluent Flow Meter at the Property Discharge Point

Install a permanent flow meter where wastewater leaves the property, so total discharge to the City pump station is a measured fact.

Glorieta Gardens is the largest single discharger into this pump station. Today, the volume leaving the property is inferred rather than measured. That is a problem in three directions at once: it makes infiltration and inflow impossible to isolate, it leaves billing and capacity allocation open to dispute, and it means any compliance question about what we discharge has no defensible answer. A meter at the exit resolves all three.

Scope of Work
  • Confirm the single point of discharge from the property to the City's collection system and verify there is no secondary or bypass connection.
  • Design a permanent metering structure — vault or dedicated manhole — sized and located for safe, permanent access and for accurate hydraulic conditions.
  • Select and specify the metering technology appropriate to the flow regime, with redundancy on the primary reading.
  • Design telemetry and continuous data logging so the record is automatic, timestamped and retrievable, not a monthly clipboard reading.
  • Permit through the City of Opa-Locka and Miami-Dade DERM as required.
  • Construct, commission and field-calibrate; establish the certified accuracy baseline.
  • Run a baseline infiltration and inflow study using the new meter — diurnal flow curves and wet-weather response — to quantify how much of what we discharge is groundwater and rainwater rather than sanitary flow.
  • Integrate the meter data feed into ProjOS for continuous monitoring and exception alerting.
Deliverables
  • Signed and sealed design drawings and specifications
  • Approved permits
  • Constructed and commissioned metering station
  • Calibration certificate and accuracy baseline
  • Baseline I&I report with wet-weather response curves
  • Live data feed into ProjOS
