Glorieta Gardens is surrounded by industrial neighbours, including junkyards. The purpose of this bucket is to establish, on the record and for the owner's own protection, whether contamination is migrating onto the property from adjacent parcels. That means an extensive file review of the surrounding properties, a plume analysis, and a monitoring well network on the boundaries — a minimum of two wells per side. Running alongside it is the standing compliance obligation: closing out the Consent Order completely and keeping water, sewer and stormwater operating in conformance with their permits at both local and state level.

Why this matters

If a neighbour's plume has reached this property, the owner needs to know it first, document it first, and control the narrative with DERM and the City — not learn about it during a transaction.

Adjacent Property Environmental File Review & Plume Assessment

Review the environmental history of every property surrounding this one — the junkyards above all — and determine where contamination could be coming from.

This is the scoping project for the entire environmental bucket, and it has to come first. The monitoring well locations, the analytical parameter list and the sampling frequency all follow from what this review finds. Placing wells before knowing what we are looking for and where it would come from means paying for wells in the wrong places, testing for the wrong things.

Scope of Work
  • Identify every adjacent and nearby property on all four sides and establish its current and historical use.
  • Focus the review on the junkyard and salvage operations, which are the most probable sources.
  • Search the regulatory databases: DERM, FDEP and EPA records — RCRA, CERCLIS, petroleum storage, dry cleaning and industrial waste files.
  • Retrieve and review enforcement history, contamination assessment reports and remediation records for each source property.
  • Review historical aerials, Sanborn maps and city directories to reconstruct site use over time.
  • Establish the contaminants of concern based on documented use and documented releases.
  • Determine the groundwater gradient and flow direction across the property from regional and site data.
  • Model probable plume vectors: which boundaries are downgradient of which sources, and where migration onto Glorieta Gardens would first appear.
Deliverables
  • Adjacent property environmental history report
  • Contaminants of concern list with the documented basis for each
  • Groundwater flow direction and gradient determination
  • Plume vector analysis with probable migration pathways
  • Monitoring well siting recommendation and analytical parameter list

ENV-02Design · Permit · ConstructNot started

Boundary Monitoring Well Network — Design & Installation

Install a monitoring well network on the property boundaries, positioned by the plume analysis.

The working assumption is a minimum of two wells on each side — four sides, so at least eight wells, with the final count and placement set by the file review. The purpose is specific and worth being precise about: these wells exist to detect contamination arriving from off-site, so that if a neighbour's plume has reached this property, the owner knows it first and documents it first.

Scope of Work
  • Finalise well count, locations and screen intervals from the plume vector analysis, with upgradient wells for background and downgradient wells for detection.
  • Design each well to DERM standards: construction, screen interval, filter pack, seal and surface completion.
  • Locate wells for permanent access and protection from vehicles and site operations.
  • Coordinate placement with the irrigation well siting so the two programs do not interfere.
  • Permit through Miami-Dade DERM.
  • Drill, install, develop and survey each well; establish measuring point elevations to NAVD 88.
  • Conduct the initial round of groundwater elevation measurements and confirm the actual flow direction against the predicted gradient.
  • Build the well network register into ProjOS with location, construction detail and sampling history.
Deliverables
  • Monitoring well network design and siting plan
  • DERM well permits
  • Installed, developed and surveyed wells with completion logs
  • Measuring point elevations tied to NAVD 88
  • Confirmed groundwater flow direction from measured elevations
  • Well network register in ProjOS

ENV-03Monitoring · AnalysisNot started

Groundwater Sampling & Plume Behaviour Analysis

Sample the wells on a defined schedule and determine whether contamination is migrating onto the property.

A single sampling round tells you a concentration. A sequence of rounds tells you a direction and a trend, and direction and trend are what actually answer the question. This project establishes the baseline and then runs the recurring rounds needed to characterise plume behaviour — whether anything is arriving, from where, at what concentration, and whether it is getting worse.

Scope of Work
  • Prepare a sampling and analysis plan with the parameter list from the file review and DERM-acceptable methods.
  • Conduct the baseline sampling round across the full network, with proper purging, field parameters and chain of custody.
  • Laboratory analysis at a Florida-certified laboratory with full quality assurance and quality control.
  • Conduct recurring rounds on the established frequency, with synoptic water level measurements each round.
  • Evaluate the results against DERM and FDEP groundwater cleanup target levels.
  • Analyse plume behaviour across rounds: presence, source attribution, concentration gradient and trend direction.
  • Determine whether contamination is migrating onto Glorieta Gardens and whether any property component has been impacted.
  • Maintain the full analytical record and trend dashboard in ProjOS.
Deliverables
  • Sampling and analysis plan
  • Baseline groundwater quality report
  • Recurring round reports with laboratory data packages
  • Plume behaviour and trend analysis
  • Comparison against groundwater cleanup target levels
  • Determination of off-site contaminant migration and property impact
  • Analytical record and trend dashboard in ProjOS

ENV-04Analysis · StrategyNot started

Environmental Findings Report & Regulatory Strategy

Convert the monitoring data into a decision: what the owner does next, and how it is presented to the City and to DERM.

Data does not protect the owner. A documented position built on data does. This project takes everything the investigation produced and turns it into a defensible finding, a recommended course of action, and a deliberate approach to the City of Opa-Locka and Miami-Dade DERM — on the owner's timing and in the owner's framing, rather than in response to somebody else's inquiry.

Scope of Work
  • Consolidate the file review, plume analysis, well installation and all sampling results into a single findings report.
  • State the conclusion on off-site contaminant migration, with the evidentiary basis for it.
  • Assess impacts, if any, on property components — soil, groundwater, utilities and structures.
  • Evaluate the regulatory implications and the owner's obligations and protections under Chapter 24 and Chapter 62-780.
  • Where a source is identified off-site, document the attribution to support the owner's position on responsibility.
  • Recommend the course of action: no further action, continued monitoring, formal notification, or assessment and remediation.
  • Develop the engagement strategy for the City of Opa-Locka and Miami-Dade DERM, including timing, framing and supporting documentation.
  • Prepare the submittal package for whichever pathway is selected.
Deliverables
  • Environmental findings report with stated conclusions
  • Property impact assessment
  • Source attribution documentation
  • Recommended course of action with alternatives evaluated
  • Regulatory engagement strategy for the City and DERM
  • Submittal package for the selected pathway

ENV-05ComplianceActive obligation

Consent Order Close-Out

Drive every enumerated corrective action to documented completion and secure written termination of the Consent Order.

This is the outcome the whole program is organised around. A Consent Order does not expire — it closes when every corrective action is complete, documented and accepted in writing. Anything less leaves the obligation live and the property encumbered. This project tracks each enumerated action to evidence-backed completion and pursues the written release.

Scope of Work
  • Build the master compliance matrix: every enumerated corrective action, its requirement, its status, the evidence of completion and the responsible party.
  • Map each corrective action to the program projects that satisfy it, so completion is traceable rather than asserted.
  • Assemble the evidence file for every action: drawings, certifications, test results, permits, photographs and correspondence.
  • Prepare and submit the periodic progress reports on the required cadence, and bring any missed reporting current.
  • Manage regulator correspondence, inspections and requests for information.
  • Identify and resolve every remaining gap between current status and full compliance.
  • Prepare and submit the close-out request with the complete evidence package.
  • Pursue written termination of the Consent Order and retain the release in the permanent record.
Deliverables
  • Master compliance matrix mapped to program projects
  • Complete evidence file for every corrective action
  • Periodic progress reports, current
  • Gap resolution plan
  • Close-out submittal package
  • Written Consent Order termination

Ongoing Regulatory Compliance Services

Keep water, sewer and stormwater operating in conformance with their permits — local, county and state — without gaps.

Compliance is not a project that finishes. Permits renew, reports come due, devices need annual certification, and a missed deadline is a violation regardless of how well the underlying system is performing. This is the standing service that keeps every obligation on a calendar with a named owner and evidence attached — and, critically, keeps the property protected in flood and storm events, which is where regulatory risk and physical risk converge.

Scope of Work
  • Maintain the master permit and obligation register across the City of Opa-Locka, Miami-Dade DERM and RER, SFWMD and FDEP.
  • Maintain the reporting calendar with lead-time alerts and a named responsible party on every submittal.
  • Prepare and submit all periodic regulatory reports across water, sewer and stormwater.
  • Manage permit renewals, modifications and condition compliance.
  • Manage annual certifications, including backflow device testing.
  • Serve as the point of contact for regulator inspections, inquiries and enforcement correspondence.
  • Monitor regulatory change at local and state level and assess its impact on the property.
  • Maintain flood-event readiness: pre-storm procedures, event response and post-event documentation and reporting.
  • Maintain the complete compliance evidence record in ProjOS, audit-ready at any time.
Deliverables
  • Master permit and obligation register
  • Reporting calendar with named responsibility and lead-time alerts
  • All periodic regulatory submittals, filed on time
  • Permit renewals and annual certifications maintained
  • Regulator correspondence file
  • Flood-event readiness and response documentation
  • Audit-ready compliance record in ProjOS
